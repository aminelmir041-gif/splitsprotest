Before you book that deal

That $1,700 aircon deal might not actually be $1,700.

And more than likely, if your home does not match the exact standard installation behind the ad, the final figure will not stay at exactly $1,700.

That does not automatically mean the quote was fake, the installer is dishonest or somebody is trying to rip you off.

Sometimes your home simply needs more work.

The useful question is not “Why is there an extra?” It is “What changed, why is it needed, and did I know about it before I committed?”

1

A $1,700 installed price can be completely genuine.

If the indoor and outdoor units are almost back-to-back, the pipe run is short, drainage is simple, access is easy and the electrical work fits inside the advertised allowance, the job may genuinely be straightforward.

Less pipe. Less cable. Less trunking. Less labour.

In that home, why should the customer pay for work they do not need?

2

But no two homes are exactly the same.

Take the exact same air conditioner and put it into two different houses.

One customer may have an outside wall directly behind the indoor unit. Another may need the outdoor unit six metres away, mounted on a bracket, with pipework routed around a corner and a longer electrical run back to the switchboard.

The air conditioner did not change.

The job did.

So should those two homes automatically cost exactly the same to install?

3

Some extras are real — and sometimes unavoidable.

Extra refrigeration pipe costs money. Cable costs money. Trunking, brackets and isolators cost money. Working at height or spending extra time finding a safe route takes labour.

Sometimes an electrical circuit genuinely needs work. Sometimes drainage cannot simply be dropped through the wall. Sometimes the outdoor unit cannot go in the easiest position.

None of that automatically makes the quote bad.

If the extra material and labour are genuinely needed for your home, somebody has to account for them somewhere.

4

The opposite can happen too.

A one-price-fits-all quote can work against the customer in the other direction.

If your job is simple but the quote already allows for a heap of pipework, difficult access or work your home does not need, you may be paying for materials and labour that never benefit you.

That is why we do not love pretending every house is the same.

You should not pay for six metres of work if your home only needs two — just like another customer should not expect six metres of work for the price of two.

5

So what should you actually look for?

You do not need a trade qualification to read a quote.

You just want to be able to answer a few simple questions:

How much pipework is included?
What electrical work is included?
What does the installer consider a standard installation?
What things could genuinely change the price at my home?
If something extra is needed, will I know the price before the work starts?

If those answers are clear, the cheapest quote may genuinely be the best deal. If they are not clear, you are still comparing numbers — not jobs.

The candid version: not every cheap aircon ad is bad, and not every higher quote is a rip-off. A simple home may need very little extra work. Another home may genuinely need hundreds of dollars more in material and labour. What matters is knowing which home you have before installation day.
Same aircon. Different job.

This is why one price rarely fits every home perfectly.

Imagine two customers buying the same 7kW system. The unit cost can be identical, while the installation behind it is completely different.

Customer A vs Customer B

Customer A
Short back-to-back pipe run. Easy drainage. Ground-level outdoor unit. Straightforward electrical run. Very little extra material or labour.
Customer B
Longer pipe run. Outdoor bracket. Extra trunking. Difficult access. Longer electrical route. More material, more time and more labour.

Same air conditioner. Two very different installation jobs.
