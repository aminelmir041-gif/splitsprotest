Before you book that deal

That $1,700 aircon deal might not stay $1,700.

A $1,700 “fully installed” deal?

It might be a great deal. Cheap does not mean bad. Expensive does not mean good.

But before you book, there is one very simple thing worth knowing:

What do you actually get for the $1,700?

Easy there, ref. We’re not calling foul. We’re just checking the replay.

An aircon job is more than the white box on the wall. Someone still has to bring it, fit it, connect it and make it work properly.

And every house is a little different.

So instead of asking, “Is $1,700 cheap?”, ask: “What is included in the $1,700?”

Because “fully installed” is doing a fair bit of heavy lifting there.

1

What are you actually paying for?

You are paying for the aircon and the job of putting it in your home.

That can mean pipe, wire, a drain, covers, electrical work, labour and testing.

Would you want to know which of those are already inside the price before you say yes?

Apparently the pipe shop still doesn’t accept good vibes as payment.

2

Same aircon. Same job?

Imagine two homes getting the exact same aircon.

Home A: the outside unit sits just behind the inside unit. Short pipe. Easy job.

Home B: the outside unit has to go much further away. More pipe. More work. More time.

Same aircon — but is that really the same job?

Same jersey. Different game.

3

So why should every home pay the same?

If your job is simple, you probably do not want to pay for a hard job you do not need.

If your job really does need more work, that extra work still costs money.

Wouldn’t it be better to know which one you have before the work starts?

You wouldn’t buy six meat pies because you only wanted two. Same idea.

4

What if $1,700 suddenly becomes $2,400?

There may be a real reason. Maybe your home needs more pipe, more electrical work or a harder install.

That is not the problem.

The problem is finding out after you thought the price was already settled.

If the price has to change, would you want to see why — and know the new price — before the extra work starts?

Extra work isn’t the villain. Surprise extra work is where the whistle comes out.

5

Four easy questions can save a lot of guessing.

You do not need to know anything about air conditioning. Just ask:

1. What do I get for this price?
Ask them to say it simply.
2. What can make the price go up?
You should know before you book.
3. Will you tell me before you do extra work?
No surprise bill at the end.
4. Is this the right size for my room?
There should be a simple reason for the size they suggest.

If someone can answer those four questions clearly, doesn’t the decision get much easier?

A decent quote should survive a replay.

Try this little test:

After reading the quote, could you explain it to your mate in 30 seconds?

If you can say what is included, what can change and when you will know the final price, you probably understand the deal.

If you need a calculator, a magnifying glass and your brother-in-law, maybe have another look.

Same aircon. Different job.

Would you pay the same for these two homes?

The aircon is the same. The work around it is not.

Easy job vs harder job

Easy job
Outside unit close by. Short pipe. Easy access. Less time.
Harder job
Outside unit further away. More pipe. More work. More time.

Same machine. Different amount of work. Should the job always cost exactly the same?

One is home before dinner. The other one packed lunch.
