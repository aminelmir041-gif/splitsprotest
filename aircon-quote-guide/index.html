Before you book that deal

That $1,700 aircon deal might not actually be $1,700.

Red flag: 7kW supplied & fully installed for $1,700

Before you decide whether that is an amazing deal, add up what the $1,700 has to cover: the 7kW unit, copper, cable, drain, trunking, electrical work and the time to install and test it properly.

Once all of that comes out of the same $1,700, how much is really left — and where do you think the saving has to come from?

Now look at your own house. Is the outdoor unit directly behind the indoor one? Is the switchboard close? Is the drain easy? Does it need a bracket? Is access simple?

If those answers change from house to house, should the final installation price always stay exactly the same?

Instead of asking “Is this quote cheap or expensive?”, try asking: “What exactly am I getting for this number?”

1

What does the advertised price actually have to cover?

Picture everything that has to arrive at your home and everything that has to happen before the aircon is running: the unit, pipe, cable, drain, trunking, electrical work, labour, testing and handover.

If one advertised number has to pay for all of that, would you want to know exactly which parts are included before you book?

2

If two homes need different work, should they cost exactly the same?

Put the same air conditioner into two houses.

House A has the outdoor unit directly behind the indoor unit, easy drainage and a simple electrical route.

House B needs six metres of pipe, extra trunking, a wall bracket, harder access and a longer electrical run.

Same air conditioner — but is it really the same job?

3

If your home only needs two metres, why would you want to pay for six?

Now turn the argument around.

If a fixed price has already allowed for long pipe runs, difficult access and extra labour, but your installation is simple, where does that extra allowance go?

Would you rather pay for the work your home actually needs, or have every possible extra built into the price whether you use it or not?

4

And if your home genuinely needs more, who pays for the extra work?

Now imagine the opposite. Your outdoor unit has to go further away. The job needs extra copper, cable and trunking. Access takes longer. A bracket is required.

Those materials have a cost. The extra time has a cost.

If your house needs substantially more than the standard job, would you expect that extra work to somehow cost nothing — or would you simply want it explained before anybody starts?

5

What would make you start asking harder questions?

Imagine you were the customer in each of these situations:

The price jumps by hundreds.
Can anyone show you what extra material or labour caused the jump?
“Fully installed” suddenly has a list of extras.
Were those exclusions clear before you booked, or did you only hear about them afterwards?
Your system size suddenly gets bigger.
Did anyone ask about room size, windows, sun, insulation, ceiling height or layout before recommending it?
You are told extra work is required.
Can the installer point to the actual reason in your home and explain it in plain English?
The price changes after work starts.
Could that issue reasonably have been identified from photos, measurements or a site check beforehand?
You are pushed to decide immediately.
Why would understanding the quote properly be a problem?

If your $1,700 job becomes $2,400 and nobody can clearly show you where the extra $700 went, what would you think?

Try this test:

Could you explain the quote to someone else after reading it? What is included? What could change? Why would it change? If you cannot answer those questions, do you really know what you are agreeing to?

Same aircon. Different job.

Would you quote these two homes the same?

Same 7kW air conditioner. Same brand. Same model. Now look at what happens around it.

Customer A vs Customer B

If the equipment is identical but one job uses more material and more time, should the installation price still be identical?

Look at the installation

Can you see why the route changes the job?

Look past the indoor unit itself. Where does the pipe go? Where can the outdoor unit sit? How far is the electrical route? How easy is the drainage?

Before you compare the number

Are you comparing prices — or are you comparing jobs?

One quote is lower. One quote is higher. But do they include the same pipe allowance? The same electrical work? The same labour? The same installation conditions?

If not, are the two numbers really comparable yet?

Can your quote answer these four questions?

What is included right now?
What could cost extra?
What would make that extra necessary?
Will I know the final price before that extra work begins?

If the answers are clear, how much easier is it to decide whether the quote is actually good value?
