Start here: correct sizing

Getting the size wrong can cost you more than you think.

Getting the sizing wrong does not just mean the room takes longer to cool. It can affect how the room feels, how hard the system has to work, costing you hundreds or even thousands over the years — and you are still not happy or comfortable.

So what is the point?

↑

Go too big

You could spend hundreds more on a bigger air conditioner, thinking you are getting something better.

You work hard for your money. That is money that could have stayed in your pocket, gone towards the kids, the mortgage, another room, or something else your family actually needs.

And after spending more, you can still end up with a worse result.

Cold air blasting directly at you
Hot, cold, hot, cold temperature changes
A room that can still feel humid or sticky
More noticeable starting, stopping and cycling
Paying for capacity your room never needed
Potentially costing you more to run over the years

So you have spent more money buying it, can spend more money running it, and the outcome still is not what you wanted.

↓

Go too small

Now let us go the other way. You save a few hundred dollars upfront. Great.

But what did you really save if the system cannot comfortably handle your home?

On an easy 20 or 25-degree day it might seem fine. Then Sydney gets a 35-degree day. The sun is beating through the windows, the house is heating up, the aircon is working flat out — and you are still sitting there uncomfortable.

The system can run for longer, work harder and still struggle to bring the room down to the temperature you expected.

Over 7, 8 or 10 years, that can mean hundreds or even thousands spent trying to get comfort from a system that was never properly suited to the space in the first place.

You saved a little when you bought it. But if you are uncomfortable every summer and the system is constantly trying to catch up, what did you actually save?

If you are spending thousands on an air conditioner you will live with for the next 7, 8 or 10 years... Wouldn't you want to know the size is actually right before you spend the money?
So how do you know?

It all depends on your home.

Two rooms can be exactly the same size on paper and still need different air conditioners. A quick square-metre rule can be a useful starting point — but it should not be the whole decision.

Think about it: if one room stays cool most of the day and another room of the same size gets hammered by afternoon sun, should they automatically get the same system?

These are the things that change the answer.

Your layout
A closed bedroom is one thing. An open-plan kitchen, dining and living area is another. If nobody asks where the air actually has to travel, part of the picture is missing.
Your windows
A room with small shaded windows can behave completely differently to the same-size room with a big glass door. The floor area is the same — the heat coming in is not.
Sun exposure
Western afternoon sun can load a room with heat for hours. If sun exposure never comes up in the sizing conversation, how accurate can the recommendation really be?
Insulation
Good insulation helps hold the comfort you have already paid to create. Poor insulation lets heat back in faster, which changes how much work the system has to do.
How many people use the room
A spare bedroom used by one person is not the same as the living room where the whole family sits together. More people means more heat in the space.
Ceiling height
The air conditioner is cooling the whole volume of air in the room, not just the square metres on the floor. Higher ceilings mean more air to control.
Before you choose a size

A few sizing mistakes are worth avoiding.

You do not need to become an air-conditioning expert. You just want enough information to know the size being recommended actually makes sense for your home.

1

Don't size it from square metres alone.

Room size is useful, but it is only the starting point. A shaded 30m² room and a 30m² room with big west-facing windows can have very different cooling needs.

If the only question you are asked is, “How many square metres is the room?”, there is still a lot about the home that has not been considered.

Same floor area does not always mean the same heat load — so why automatically choose the same aircon?

2

Don't copy what someone else has.

Your neighbour might love their 7kW. Your brother might have a 5kW in a room that looks similar. That still does not mean either size is right for your place.

Their windows, insulation, sun exposure, ceiling height and layout may be completely different.

You are paying for comfort in your home — not theirs.

3

Don't automatically “go bigger to be safe”.

It sounds logical: if 5kW might work, buy 7kW and you cannot go wrong.

But you can end up paying hundreds more for capacity you never needed, with stronger blasts of cold air, more noticeable cycling and a room that may still not feel as comfortable as you expected.

If the bigger unit costs more, shouldn't it also have a clear reason for being there?

4

Don't go too small just to save a few hundred dollars.

A smaller system can look like the cheaper option on the day you buy it.

But when Sydney gets a genuinely hot afternoon and the system is running flat out while the room is still uncomfortable, that saving can stop feeling very clever.

Saving a little upfront is not much of a saving if you spend years wishing the system had enough power.

5

If you're not sure, don't guess.

You are not expected to know whether your room needs 3.5kW, 5kW or 7kW. That is exactly why a few photos, room measurements and simple questions about the home are useful.

And if somebody gives you a size before asking about the windows, sun, insulation, layout or ceiling height, ask them how they arrived at it.

A good recommendation should be easy to explain.
