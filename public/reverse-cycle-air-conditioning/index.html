Reverse Cycle Air Conditioning in Sydney

Reverse cycle means the same air conditioner can heat your home in winter and cool it in summer. Both wall-mounted splits and ducted systems can do this. Your room layout determines how the air reaches you.

What changes when you switch from cool to heat?

In cooling mode, the system moves heat from inside the house to the outdoor air. Heating reverses that process. On a cold morning, the indoor fan on some models waits until the coil is warm. The system may also pause heating briefly to defrost the outdoor coil. Your model’s operating guide describes its normal indicators.

Read the Sydney figures on the energy label

On a Zoned Energy Rating Label, Sydney is in the average climate zone. Look at the heating and cooling figures separately. Two units with similar cooling performance can have different winter energy use. Start by comparing units of a suitable capacity for the room.

The kW figure on the front of a quote usually describes heating or cooling capacity. For a running-cost comparison, find the label’s annual electricity use in kWh and multiply it by your electricity usage rate. For example, 500 kWh a year at 30 cents per kWh works out to $150 a year for that part of the label. This is an illustration. Your hours of use, set temperature, insulation and tariff change the result.

Choose for the hardest room to keep comfortable

A west-facing lounge with afternoon sun may drive summer sizing. A shaded bedroom used overnight may put more weight on quiet heating at low fan speed. Record room dimensions, glazing, ceiling height and which adjoining spaces stay open. Use our air conditioner sizing calculator for a starting point.
